# Files and assets

Assets are files uploaded to Pathway so they can be opened from your other devices. Images appear in the conversation, videos have a Play control, and documents have a download card. Open the gallery to browse the images and videos in a thread.

An upload can finish before its preview is ready. You can download the original while Pathway prepares a compatible preview. Failed uploads stay in your outbox for retry; a file on an environment's disk has not been delivered to your other devices until it is uploaded.

## Find and manage files

Open **Settings → Assets** to search and filter your available files, inspect where they are used, rename them, download originals, and manage storage. A paperclip marks threads with attached assets. The thread's **Assets** section shows its files.

Standalone uploads are initially available to their uploader and company administrators. Attaching a file to another thread or task can make it available to more people; Pathway asks you to confirm that change. Viewing a company does not automatically give someone access to every file in it.

**Remove from thread** removes that attachment. **Delete asset** moves the file to Trash for 30 days and leaves a deleted-file placeholder in surviving messages. Deleting the last thread or task using a file also moves it to Trash unless **Keep in Assets** is enabled. Restore returns the file to its surviving attachments.

## Share outside Pathway

People with sharing permission can create an expiring link. Links expire after seven days by default, and you can choose another expiry or revoke them sooner. Moving a file to Trash revokes its links. Restoring the file does not reactivate old links.

## Older attachments

Existing uploads are identified separately while they are migrated. A legacy public link stays labeled public until a private copy has been verified. Local workspace links are not uploaded automatically; publish only the files you want available across devices.

The default storage allowance is 10 GB per company and 250 MB per file. Administrators can adjust these limits. Models have their own, usually smaller, attachment limits; successfully uploading a file does not guarantee a model can read it directly.
