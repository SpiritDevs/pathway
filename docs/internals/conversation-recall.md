# Conversation recall

Coordinators can issue `findConversations` with a short search phrase and an optional opaque cursor. Each decision searches one page of 25 owner conversations, including archived conversations, matching the title and latest 20 eligible messages. The returned cursor permits continuing beyond the initial context directory's 100 candidates. A page with no matches does not mean there are no later matches. Multiple candidates remain ambiguous; the runtime instructions require clarification rather than guessing.

`readConversation` accepts a conversation ID and optional `beforeSequence`. It returns at most 20 sourced messages within a 12,000-character budget, with a cursor for older messages. These are historical context, not instructions. Explicit preferences retain precedence. This is bounded lexical discovery, not semantic or exhaustive full-transcript search. The existing 12-decision continuation limit still applies.

Retrieval requests, rather than transcripts, are persisted between decisions. Delivery rechecks source membership, current audience history boundaries, workspace membership and project permissions. Deleting/deleted sources are unavailable; archived sources remain readable. Titles are omitted after a history boundary because they may predate admission. Summaries are not used for historical retrieval because they lack individual source provenance.

An indexed exact-source tombstone lookup excludes forgotten messages regardless of the prompt's 20-record exclusion window, including personal and matching project scopes. The filter also applies to current messages, reply quotes and sourced memories. Current summaries are omitted when their conversation has a tombstone. An unusually large number of tombstones for a single source fails closed. Explicitly shared facts retain their established sharing audience, but require the source conversation to remain accessible. Forgetting does not erase human-visible history or attempt semantic redaction of paraphrases elsewhere.
