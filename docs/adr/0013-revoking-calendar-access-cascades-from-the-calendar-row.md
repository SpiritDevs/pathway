# Revoking calendar access cascades from the calendar row

Revoking a grant tombstones the `calendar` row for that grantee and the client drops every event belonging to it, because events are keyed by `calendarId` and calendar-level cascade costs exactly one feed row. The alternatives all fail on volume: bumping the company-wide `authorizationEpoch` — the mechanism role-assignment writes use — would force _every_ member of the company to discard and reseed their replica on every unshare, and calendar sharing churns far more than role assignment ever does; tombstoning each event individually emits thousands of rows per revoke and, under the narrowing-tombstone rule, broadcasts them wider than the audience being narrowed. Disconnecting a `calendarAccount` cascades the same way through its calendars, events, and grants. `calendarEventLink` rows survive both, because they are keyed by stable Google event ids: the issue shows "event unavailable" and reconnecting restores the link rather than silently losing it. The cascade relies on the client honoring it, which is acceptable because no option can un-send data already replicated to disk; a per-member epoch is the upgrade path if hard revocation is ever required.
